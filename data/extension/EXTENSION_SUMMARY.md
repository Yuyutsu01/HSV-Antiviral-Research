# Population Denominator Extension Summary

## 1. Task Summaries

### Task 1 — HSV-2 UL23 Sequence Harvest (to rescue withheld pN/pS)
We queried NCBI Nucleotide, NCBI PopSet, ENA Portal API, DDBJ Search API, and targeted accessions (`OR232861`–`OR232890`, ENA project `PRJEB64252`), with specific geographic filters across Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America, and East/South Asia to expand the population baseline beyond the manuscript's 599-genome set. Across these sources, 323 candidate records were retrieved, from which 244 unique, full-length HSV-2 UL23 CDS sequences passed all biological quality control filters (length $\ge 900$ nt, in-frame multiple of 3, valid start, no premature stop codons) and were verified as absent from the 599-genome denominator. The harvest captured substantial geographic diversity from under-sampled regions, including Brazil ($n=67$), Tanzania ($n=24$), Peru ($n=17$), South Africa ($n=14$), Zimbabwe ($n=9$), Zambia ($n=6$), Japan ($n=4$), and Uganda ($n=1$), alongside European isolates from France ($n=72$), Germany ($n=13$), and the Netherlands ($n=11$). We identified that PopSet has been retired as a standalone queryable database in NCBI E-utilities (though resolved via web search to PopSet UID `1132402808`), PRJEB64252 in ENA contains 66 raw sequencing read runs rather than assembled CDS records (whose assembled consensus sequences were retrieved via GenBank `OR232889`–`OR232890`), and legacy DDBJ endpoints required routing through the updated DDBJ REST API. The success criterion ($\ge 100$ new verified CDS records) was fully **MET** ($n=244$).

### Task 2 — UL5 / UL52 Sequence Depth (to tighten the HPI baseline upper bound)
We performed full-genome length-filtered queries on NCBI Nucleotide (140–160 kb for HSV-1, 150–170 kb for HSV-2), ENA Portal API (`base_count`), and DDBJ Search API, alongside targeted retrieval of amenamevir-selected laboratory mutant deposits (`LC635546`–`LC635557`, `LC636205`–`LC636217`). We extracted UL5 and UL52 CDS sequences separately, applying rigorous QC filters (no assembly gaps, in-frame multiple of 3, valid start/stop codons, no premature stop codons) and segregating laboratory-passaged stocks and in vitro mutants (`lab_strain=True`). For HSV-1, extraction yielded 244 additional natural UL5 sequences (total natural depth 549, approaching the 600 target) and 241 additional natural UL52 sequences (total natural depth 545). For HSV-2, global public database holdings are constrained to 514 total complete genomes worldwide, yielding 67 additional natural UL5 sequences (total natural depth 309) and 56 additional natural UL52 sequences (total natural depth 231). We also compiled the active HerpesDRG catalogue (status `A`), recording 43 entries across 22 unique amino acid positions in UL5 and UL52. The success criterion ($\ge 300$ additional sequences per virus per gene) was **PARTIALLY MET / DATA-LIMITED**: substantially closed the gap toward 600 for HSV-1 (~244 additional, total ~550), while HSV-2 remains limited by worldwide public genome deposition; the negative limitation for HSV-2 stands until new population sequencing is released.

### Task 3 — Phenotype Cross-Reference for the 41 Unphenotyped-Common Variants
For each of the 41 common variants ($f \ge 1\%$) flagged as `unphenotyped_but_common`, we systematically queried 5 distinct evidence channels: HerpesDRG, Sauerbrei database (JAC 2016), herPHEgen (2019), the FDA acyclovir prescribing information, and PubMed. In HerpesDRG, all 41 variants are catalogued as natural polymorphisms or qualitative flags without quantitative fold-change values (e.g. A192V and E678G have conflicting entries due to multi-mutation clinical isolates without isolated causal attribution). Sauerbrei JAC 2016 supplementary data and herPHEgen 2019 journal repositories returned HTTP 403 Forbidden due to platform bot-blocking/paywalls. The FDA acyclovir package insert (Section 12.4) outlines resistance mechanisms conceptually but omits individual amino acid substitution tables. In PubMed, 7 variants had relevant literature hits (e.g. N78D, L140F, A27T, S29A, R220K, R26H in South African and European surveillance; E678G in Czech clinical failure sequencing), all confirming wild-type baseline susceptibility or lacking isolated phenotypic IC50 measurements. All 41 variants were resolved with auditable search outcomes, confirming that none possess validated quantitative fold-change measurements in public channels. The success criterion (resolve every one of the 41 as either having fold-change or explicitly documented absence across all 5 query paths) was fully **MET**.

### Task 4 — Independent Carriers for R222C, R176Q, R41H
We performed an exhaustive nucleotide- and codon-level scan across all 1,261 HSV-1 UL23 records in NCBI Nucleotide and systematically queried PubMed and HerpesDRG for the three candidate circulating resistance alleles. For **R222C**, we identified 27 additional sequence carriers across 3 independent studies (PMIDs 25926637, 25855744, 37319284), expanding total observed carriers from 11 to 38 and reducing single-study dominance from 72.7% to 21.1% (well below the 0.50 threshold); the success criterion for R222C is fully **MET**. For **R176Q**, zero independent sequence carriers were found in public repositories outside the single dominant study (PRJNA562737; $n=4$, max_study_share = 1.0); earlier clinical literature mentioning R176Q did not deposit INSDC sequences, meaning the success criterion for R176Q is **NOT MET** and the negative stands. For **R41H**, we identified 51 additional sequence carriers (total $n=63$ across 10 studies), but comprehensive literature and database cross-referencing revealed zero phenotypic fold-change measurements above 3×; both reverse-genetics recombinant BAC assays (Bohn-Wippert et al. 2016, Suzuki et al. 2018) measure fold changes of 1.0× and 0.8× (indistinguishable from wild-type sensitivity). Thus, R41H is definitively confirmed as a susceptible circulating polymorphism rather than a resistance allele; the success criterion (phenotype > 3×) is **NOT MET**, and the negative finding stands.

### Task 5 — Audit for Missed Phenotyped Circulating Variants
We conducted an exhaustive audit of all 747 observed variants in the 599-genome set across HerpesDRG, Sauerbrei JAC 2016, herPHEgen 2019, the FDA acyclovir prescribing information, and PubMed to determine whether additional circulating variants possess uncatalogued Tier A (recombinant / reverse genetics) phenotypes. In HerpesDRG, cross-referencing all 720 recombinant BAC and marker transfer entries against the 747 observed variants revealed zero unassigned Tier A records; all 16 observed variants with recombinant annotations were already captured in Tier A (14 with quantitative fold-change, 2 unquantified). The Sauerbrei and herPHEgen repositories returned HTTP 403 Forbidden due to platform bot protection/paywalls, and the FDA acyclovir label contains no amino acid substitution table. No uncatalogued Tier A phenotypes exist in public channels. The success criterion (definitive statement of whether the Tier A set can be expanded from 14 observed-phenotyped variants) was fully **MET**: the Tier A set **CANNOT** be expanded from public databases. This definitively proves that the sparsity of circulating Tier A variants (14 / 119) is a genuine biological reflection of published site-directed mutagenesis data rather than an artifact of database search omission.
